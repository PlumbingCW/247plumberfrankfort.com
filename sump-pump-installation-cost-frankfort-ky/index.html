Sump Pump Installation Cost in Frankfort, KY: What Changes the Price

Prices vary with the pit, the pump and the discharge. This guide lists the real cost drivers so you can compare quotes.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Frankfort 24/7 Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

People who search for the cost of a sump pump installation often want one number. No honest single number exists, because a basement with an existing pit and a working discharge is a very different job from one that needs a new pit cut into the slab. This guide explains what each part of the job involves and how it changes the price, so that quotes from different plumbers can be compared line by line.

We do not publish dollar figures here because they change with equipment and labor, and a number printed on a web page is out of date quickly. What stays true is the list of questions that determine the price. If you can answer them before you call, the quote comes faster and is more accurate.

Pit work is often the biggest variable

If a pit and pump already exist and you are replacing the pump, the job is quick. If you are installing the first pump in a basement, the crew must cut the slab, dig a hole for the liner, set the liner, run a drain connection and patch the floor. That is a day of work, with a mess to clean and a floor that needs time to cure.

The location of the pit matters. A pit placed where water naturally collects works better than one placed for convenience. We look at where water enters and how the floor slopes, then choose the spot.

Questions that make your quote more accurate

  • Is there already a pit in the floor, and does it have a pump in it now?
  • Where does the discharge currently go, and how far is it from the house to the point of discharge?
  • Does the basement have a floor drain or an interior drain system that could connect?
  • Has the basement flooded in a storm before, and how high did the water get?
  • Is there a working outlet nearby on a circuit with ground fault protection?
  • Do you want a battery backup or a water-powered backup?

Backup systems are worth pricing from the start

A storm that fills a sump pit often takes the power with it. A battery backup pump takes over when the main pump stops or the power fails. A water-powered backup uses pressure from the water supply to lift water and runs without electricity, though it uses water while it runs and needs the right plumbing setup.

Adding the backup at the same visit as the main pump costs less than adding it later, because the pit, the check valves and the discharge are already open. If you plan to finish the basement or store things you cannot afford to lose, plan the backup at the same time as the main pump.

Discharge: where the water goes matters

Water pumped out of the pit has to go somewhere that is allowed and that does not send it back. Discharge to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed, since groundwater overloads the sewer system. Discharge near the foundation sends the water back into the soil that drains to the pit, and it cycles over and over.

The best discharge runs far enough from the house to ground that slopes away, and has a way to avoid freezing in winter. A plumber can advise on options, and you can ask your local building department about rules for your address. Frankfort rain and river flooding, in context

Frankfort receives about 47 inches of precipitation in a typical year at Capital City Airport, according to the 1991 to 2020 climate normals, and the Kentucky River has flooded the city many times. The record crest of 48.47 feet in December 1978 put more than half of downtown under water, according to FEMA's high water mark pilot summary. In April 2025, high water again forced evacuations.

A sump pump handles groundwater and seepage. It does not keep river floodwater out. If your home is in a flood area, ask your insurance agent about flood coverage and ask local officials which rules apply to your lot. Questions we hear about sump pump installation cost

Often one quote includes a new pit, a backup system or a long discharge, and the other does not. Compare the scope line by line before comparing the totals.

Replacing a pump in an existing pit is usually under half a day. A new pit with a discharge run can take a day or more.

Policies vary. Some cover damage from a pump failure only if you bought a water backup endorsement. Ask your carrier.

Rules depend on the work and the address. Ask your local building department whether a permit applies before work begins.

If water enters your basement after rain, a pump and drain system is the standard response. If the cause is a plumbing leak or a river flood, a different fix applies.
