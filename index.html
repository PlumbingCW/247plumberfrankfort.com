Emergency plumbing

Water where it should not be? Start here.

An emergency plumber in Frankfort, KY is the person you call when waiting until morning is not an option. A pipe is spraying, a toilet is overflowing, sewage is coming up a basement drain or the water heater has failed on the coldest night of the week. Our dispatch line is open every hour of every day, and the first thing we do is tell you how to limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

For a burst pipe, close the main valve, open a low faucet and call. For a backed-up drain, stop using water. For a leaking water heater, close the cold inlet and switch off the power or the gas control. Our burst pipe repair page and our drain cleaning page walk through each case in more detail.

Old city, older pipes

Plumbing in the Kentucky capital

Frankfort was founded in 1786 and has been the state capital since Kentucky became a state in 1792. The Capitol area, the Corner in Celebrities Historic District and South Frankfort hold houses and buildings that are far older than the plastic pipe now used in new construction. Clay, cast iron, galvanized steel and decades of mixed repairs are all part of the story under those streets.

That age matters when a drain keeps clogging or a supply line starts to leak. We use a camera on repeat drain problems so a repair targets the real cause, and we explain which fixes can wait and which cannot. If you are buying an older home, a sewer inspection before closing is a sensible step.

Weather and water

Cold snaps, heavy rain and river water

Frankfort's average January low is about 25 degrees Fahrenheit at the airport and a few degrees colder downtown, and the record lows are well below zero. A hard freeze after a mild stretch is when pipes in exterior walls and crawl spaces split. Our burst pipe repair crews know the pattern, and we help you prevent a repeat.

The Kentucky River has flooded the city many times. The record crest of 48.47 feet in December 1978 put more than half of downtown under water. After heavy rain, a sump pump that works matters, and so does a discharge that leads water away from the house. A sump pump handles groundwater, not river floods, so ask your insurance agent about flood coverage.

Hot water

Water heaters: repair, replace or upgrade

A water heater that stops working at night is one of the most common emergency calls. We diagnose gas, electric and hybrid heaters, repair failed parts and replace tanks that have corroded. Our water heater replacement visits include removal of the old tank and testing of the new one, and we explain the timing when you call.

Planning ahead is cheaper than waiting for a flood. If your heater is past its typical life, ask about water heater installation or a tankless water heater. Frankfort Plant Board's water quality report does not state hardness, so ask the utility or test your tap, and flush a tank heater once a year either way.

What happens when you call

A person answers and asks what is happening, where you are and whether water is still flowing. Those questions are not small talk. They let dispatch decide whether to send a plumber with a drain machine, a camera, a water heater or a set of pipe tools, so the first truck carries what the job needs.

While you wait, we talk you through the immediate steps. That may be closing a valve, moving belongings or switching off power in a dry panel. You get an arrival window instead of a promise of an exact minute, because traffic, weather and other emergencies change the clock.

On arrival, the plumber finds the cause, explains it in plain language and gives you a price before starting. If a repair is not worth making, you hear that too. Our services page lists the work we do, and each page explains the repair and what changes the price.

Habits that keep a house out of trouble

Know where your main water shutoff is and test it once a year. A valve that has not moved in a decade may not close when you need it, and replacing it on a calm day is cheaper than finding out during a leak.

Keep grease out of the kitchen drain, use hair catchers in tubs and flush only toilet paper. Disconnect garden hoses before the first freeze and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Flush the water heater once a year and look under every sink twice a year for damp cabinets.

If you are buying an older house in Frankfort, ask for a camera look at the sewer line before closing. A short inspection shows what a standard home inspection cannot.

Choosing a plumber in Kentucky

Kentucky licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, Division of Plumbing, under 815 KAR 20:030. Ask any plumber for a license and verify it with the department. Ask who pulls any required permit and how the work is inspected. A good plumber answers these questions without hesitation.

If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and your gas utility. Columbia Gas of Kentucky lists its 24/7 emergency number as 1-800-432-9515. Call before you dig, too. Kentucky 811 marks buried utilities for free.
