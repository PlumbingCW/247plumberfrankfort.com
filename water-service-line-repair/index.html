Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Frankfort, KY

No water, low pressure, a soggy patch in the yard or a meter that will not stop spinning can all point to the buried service line.

Call (855) 575-2890

Emergency water service line repair in Frankfort, KY covers the buried pipe that brings water from the meter to your house. When it breaks, you may lose water completely, see pressure fall, find a wet or sunken spot in the yard, or watch the meter run with every tap closed. Close the shutoff valve at the house, check the meter and call our 24/7 line.

Frankfort Plant Board owns and maintains the water main and the meter. The line from the meter to your house belongs to the property owner. That boundary matters, because a leak on the utility side is the utility's to fix, and a leak on your side is yours.

According to Frankfort Plant Board's published water quality report, the supply is treated Kentucky River water, but the report does not say where each customer's line ends, so we confirm the boundary with the utility. Our crews find the leak, confirm which side it is on, and repair the part that belongs to you.

Suspect a buried line leak: what to do

  • Close every tap, then watch the meter or its leak indicator. If it moves, water is going somewhere.
  • Shut the main valve at the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house.
  • Look for wet ground, a soft or sunken area, a green strip in dry weather or water bubbling up near the meter box.
  • Do not dig around the meter box. Call the Kentucky 811 service before any digging so buried utilities are marked.
  • Call us with the meter reading and what you see. If the water is off, leave the main closed until we arrive.

How we find a leak you cannot see

A buried line leaks out of sight, so the first step is to confirm the leak with a meter test and a pressure test. Next, listening equipment on the pipe follows the sound of escaping water along the route. A locator marks the line and the loudest point. That narrows the dig to a few feet of ground instead of the whole yard.

After that we excavate the spot, expose the pipe and cut out the failed section. If the pipe is galvanized, lead-suspect or past its life, we talk to you about replacing the whole run instead of splicing one spot.

Where the water comes from in Frankfort

Frankfort Plant Board treats surface water drawn from pool 4 of the Kentucky River at its plant on Coffeetree Road, and distributes it through mains to customers across the city. The utility's water quality report lists chloramines as the disinfectant. The report does not give a hardness figure, so ask the Plant Board if you need it for a water heater or softener decision.

River flooding, such as the record high water of December 1978 and the April 2025 event, shifts ground and stresses buried lines. If your yard was saturated or covered by high water, check the meter afterward for movement and look for wet spots over the line.

From the call to the finished repair

  1. Confirm and isolate

    We shut off the supply and verify the leak is on the customer side of the meter.

  2. Call 811 and locate

    We make sure utilities are marked before any digging, and we locate the leak with listening gear.

  3. Excavate and repair

    We open the smallest hole that exposes the pipe, cut out the damage and install new pipe and fittings.

  4. Test and backfill

    We pressurize the line, check for leaks and backfill the trench in layers.

  5. Restore

    We return the area to an agreed condition and explain what to watch for.
