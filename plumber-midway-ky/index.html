Plumbing in Kentucky's first railroad town

Midway began in 1835 when the Lexington and Ohio Railroad bought a farm to create a town between Lexington and Frankfort. It was first called Middleway and was renamed Midway in 1837. The railroad still runs through the middle of town, and the street grid grew up around it.

A town of that age has older pipes. Homes may carry cast iron or clay drains, galvanized or copper supply lines and additions made at different times. A basement, a crawl space or a porch addition may hide a section of pipe that nobody has seen in decades. We find the cause before we repair it, so the fix is the right size.

If your drain keeps clogging in a house of that age, a camera is the next step. Our sewer inspection page explains what the recording shows and when it is worth the cost.

Fire, water and the bourbon history

A distillery stood at the corner of Gratz and Dudley Streets from 1865 until 1959, and a warehouse fire on August 4, 1908 destroyed more than 47,000 barrels of bourbon. Two of the old buildings still stand, and Lee's Branch of North Elkhorn Creek runs nearby. Those details are part of why Midway pays attention to water and drainage near the creek.

Homes near a creek or a low corner can see groundwater rise after heavy rain. A sump pump and a clear discharge line are the first line of defense, and a backup protects a pit through a power outage.
