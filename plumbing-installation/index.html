Plumbing Installation in Frankfort, KY

Planning a remodel or adding a fixture? We install and connect plumbing to code and test it before we leave.

Call (855) 575-2890

Plumbing installation in Frankfort, KY covers new sinks, toilets, tubs, showers, faucets, shutoff valves, washer hookups, ice makers, outdoor spigots and the supply and drain lines behind them. Remodels, additions and basement finishes often need a new drain, a new vent and a new supply, and a plan up front saves time later.

This page is for planned work. If something is leaking or backing up now, our emergency pages for leak detection, drain cleaning and burst pipe repair apply and the 24/7 line is open.

Plan the drain and vent before the fixtures

Every fixture drain needs a trap, a path with the right slope to the main and a vent so the trap seal is not siphoned. A fixture added in the wrong place can drain slowly or gurgle. The design decisions, such as pipe size and where the vent ties in, are made before the walls are closed.

In a basement, the bathroom may sit below the level of the main drain. In that case a sewage ejector pump lifts waste to the main. It adds a pump and a sealed pit to the project, so it belongs in the price from the start.

  5. Final test

    We check every fixture for flow, drainage and leaks and walk you through the shutoffs.

License and permits in Kentucky

Kentucky licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, Division of Plumbing, under 815 KAR 20:030. Ask any contractor for the license and verify it through the department. Permit requirements depend on the job and the location, so ask your local building department what applies to your address. Model plumbing standards such as the International Plumbing Code and the Uniform Plumbing Code govern traps and vents, and states adopt and amend them.

Gas piping has its own care. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 and your gas utility. Columbia Gas of Kentucky lists its 24/7 emergency number as 1-800-432-9515. Check your bill to confirm your gas provider.

Supply sizing and water pressure on a new fixture

Every fixture added to a house shares the same supply pipe. A new tub with a large filler or a shower with several heads can draw more water than a small supply line carries, and the result is weak pressure at every other fixture when someone showers. We check the size of the supply line and the pressure at the meter before we add anything large.

A pressure regulator that is set too high wears out faucets and supply lines early, while one that is set too low makes a shower feel weak. A simple gauge on a hose bib shows the pressure in a minute, and we adjust or replace the regulator when the number is off.

Hot water has its own limits. A house with a small water heater may not support a large new tub, so the project sometimes includes a heater upgrade. See water heater installation for how that works.

Fixtures worth planning before the walls close

Some things are cheap to add during a remodel and costly to add afterward. A shutoff valve for every fixture, an access panel behind a tub, a hose bib that is frost free and a floor drain in a laundry room all fall in that group. We suggest them at the walkthrough so they appear in the written price.

Rough-in dimensions are the other early decision. The distance from the wall to the toilet drain, the height of a shower valve and the position of a vanity drain have to match the fixtures you picked. A mismatch found after the tile goes up means opening the wall again.
