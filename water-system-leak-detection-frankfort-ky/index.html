Water System Leak Detection: A Frankfort, KY Homeowner Guide

A leak in the water system wastes money before it causes damage. A few simple tests tell you whether you have one.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Frankfort 24/7 Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

A water system leak is a leak in the pipes that bring water into and through your house. Unlike a drain leak, it is under pressure, so it runs all the time and wastes water around the clock. The first sign is often a bill that rises with no change in habits, or a meter that moves when you are not using water.

This guide shows how to run a meter test at home, how to check the usual suspects like toilets and hose bibs and when a plumber with listening equipment is the right next step. It also covers what to do about a leak you find.

The meter test, step by step

  1. Turn everything off

    Close every faucet, stop the washer and dishwasher and make sure no one flushes a toilet.

  2. Read the meter

    Open the meter box and note the reading, or watch the small leak indicator, a triangle or a dial that spins when water flows.

  3. Wait

    Wait fifteen to thirty minutes without using water.

  4. Read it again

    If the reading changed or the indicator moved, water is going somewhere.

  5. Close the main

    Turn off the main valve inside the house and check the meter again. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps running, the leak is between the meter and the house.

Hidden leaks and how they are found

A leak inside a wall, under a slab or in a buried line needs tools to find. Pressure testing isolates sections of the system. Acoustic equipment listens for the hiss of water escaping from a pipe. Thermal cameras reveal temperature differences where hot water leaks, and moisture meters find wet material behind finished surfaces.

The aim is to narrow the leak to a small area before opening anything. After the repair, we test again to confirm the meter is still. See our leak detection page for the full list of what we do.

Clues that point to a hidden leak

  • Water bill higher than usual with no change in habits.
  • Sound of running water when everything is off.
  • Damp spots, stains or bubbling paint on walls and ceilings.
  • Warm spots on the floor, which can mean a hot water line under a slab.
  • Musty smells or mold in an area that should be dry.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house.

What to do once you find a leak

Close the nearest shutoff. Take photographs of the damage and the leak for your insurance. Dry what you can.

Call a plumber for a repair and, for any significant water damage, a drying company. Mold can start in a day or two in a damp wall, so speed matters.

If the leak is on the service line from the meter to the house, it is typically the homeowner's responsibility, and our water service line repair crew handles it. If the water runs through a pipe that is failing in many places, a repiping quote may be the better long-term answer.

Seasonal checks that catch leaks early

Check under every sink and behind the toilets twice a year, and look at the ceiling below any upstairs bathroom. Run your hand along supply lines and feel for moisture. A dry paper towel held against a joint shows a slow drip that your eyes miss.

Check the water heater, the washing machine hoses and the ice maker line at the same time. Rubber washer hoses fail without warning, and replacing them with braided stainless hoses every few years is cheap protection. A water alarm placed on the floor near the heater or the washer gives an early warning for the cost of a few dollars.

Reading a Frankfort Plant Board bill and meter

Frankfort Plant Board bills customers for water by usage, and its meters register flow with a dial or digital display. Your bill lists the usage for the period, so a rise from one month to the next is a clue. If you cannot find a leak, ask the utility whether it can check the meter reading and whether it offers any help or adjustment after a leak, since policies differ and we do not assume one.

The utility's water quality report states the source as pool 4 of the Kentucky River and the plant on Coffeetree Road. Towns around Frankfort, such as Versailles and Midway, have their own providers, and our pages for Versailles and Midway explain how we serve them.
