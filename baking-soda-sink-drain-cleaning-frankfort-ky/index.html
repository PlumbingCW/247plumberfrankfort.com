Baking Soda for Cleaning Sink Drains: What Works in Frankfort, KY

Baking soda freshens and loosens light film in a slow drain. It will not clear a real clog. Here is the difference.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Frankfort 24/7 Plumbing team. Published 2026-11-16.

Pouring baking soda and vinegar down a sink is one of the most common home drain remedies, and it has a real but narrow use. It can loosen a thin layer of soap film and grease and reduce odors. It cannot dissolve a hair clog, a grease plug or a stuck object. Knowing which problem you have saves a lot of frustration.

This guide explains the chemistry in plain terms, gives a safe method for a mild slow drain, and lists the signs that mean you should stop experimenting and call a plumber. If water is already rising in the sink, skip ahead to the section on when to call.

What baking soda and vinegar actually do

Baking soda is a mild base and vinegar is a mild acid. When they meet, they fizz and release carbon dioxide. The foam can help lift loose film from the walls of a pipe, and the mild base can neutralize some odor-causing residue. The effect is gentle and short.

The fizzing does not generate enough force to push a clog through a pipe, and it does not break down hair, food solids or a hardened grease plug. A fully blocked drain will not drain the mixture, so the foam simply sits in the sink. Plastic pipe makers also publish guidelines for the hottest water their pipe should carry, so check yours before you pour anything hot.

A safe method for a slow, smelly drain

  1. Clear the sink

    Remove standing water with a cup and take out the stopper or strainer. Clean off visible hair and debris by hand with gloves.

  2. Pour in baking soda

    Add about half a cup of baking soda down the drain, working it in with a spoon.

  3. Add vinegar

    Pour in about a cup of white vinegar, and cover the drain with a plug or a cloth for a few minutes so the foam works in the pipe.

  4. Flush with hot tap water

    Run hot water, not boiling, for a minute or two. Boiling water can soften and damage plastic pipe and fittings.

  5. Repeat only once

    If the drain is still slow, the problem is not light film, and more baking soda will not help.

What not to do

  • Do not mix baking soda and vinegar with commercial drain cleaners. Combining chemicals can release harmful fumes.
  • Do not use boiling water on plastic drain pipes or fittings, or on a cracked porcelain sink.
  • Do not use a chemical opener in a fully blocked drain. It sits in the standing water and is hazardous to the person who opens the pipe next.
  • Do not use a metal coat hanger on a drain with a garbage disposal. You can damage the unit or injure yourself.
  • Do not keep experimenting with a sink that is rising toward the rim. Stop and call.

Habits that keep sink drains clear

Use a mesh strainer in the kitchen sink and the tub, and empty it into the trash. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. Pour cooled cooking fat into a can and throw it away with the trash. Flush the kitchen drain with hot tap water for a minute after washing dishes.

In bathrooms, a hair catcher in the tub or shower makes the biggest difference. Pull it out and clean it weekly. If your kitchen sink has a disposal, remember it is for soft scraps, and run cold water as you use it. See garbage disposal repair if yours jams.

If the same drain keeps slowing, the cause may be a rough pipe, a sag or roots, and it is time for a camera inspection. Our hydro jetting page explains how heavy buildup is cleared.

Where Frankfort drain water goes

Inside Frankfort, household drains connect to the city sewer system and the line from the house to the connection is generally the property owner's. In towns around Frankfort, such as Versailles, Lawrenceburg and Midway, the provider and the rules can differ. Ask your sewer provider where its responsibility ends and yours begins before you start a repair. See our pages for Lawrenceburg and Midway for the service we offer outside Frankfort.

Older Frankfort neighborhoods, such as South Frankfort and the Corner in Celebrities Historic District, have houses built when drains were made of materials that age differently from modern plastic. Slow drains in an old house are worth a camera look sooner than in a new one.
