Water heater installation in Frankfort, KY is the planned job of putting in a new tank, tankless or hybrid unit, whether you are replacing an aging heater before it fails, remodeling, or moving the unit to a better spot. A good install matters as much as the heater itself, because most early failures trace back to poor venting, missing expansion control or an undersized unit.

This page covers a planned install. If your tank is already leaking, see emergency water heater replacement, where the same crews handle urgent calls.

Sizing a heater to the household

The label capacity is only half the story. What counts is how much hot water the house needs at its busiest hour. A family that showers in the morning and runs a dishwasher and washer at the same time needs more recovery than two people living alone. We ask how many bathrooms you have, how many people use them in the same window, and whether there is a soaking tub or a body spray shower.

A tank that is too small runs out of hot water and runs the burner constantly. A tank that is far too big wastes energy. We size to the real load and tell you why.

What a clean install looks like

  1. Walk the site

    We look at the location, the existing connections, the venting path and the shutoff valves before we quote.

  2. Prepare the area

    Old tank out, a drain pan where needed, a level base, and a discharge line from the relief valve that runs where it is safe.

  3. Connect and protect

    New connectors, a shutoff valve within reach, an expansion tank where the system needs one, and a relief valve rated for the tank.

  4. Fuel and venting

    Gas connections get a leak test. Vent pipe is sloped and sealed. Electrical connections are checked and labeled.

  5. Start up

    We fill, purge air, set the temperature, run hot water and walk you through shutoff points.

Gas safety and permits in plain terms

A gas water heater shares your home with a flame and a flue, so the venting and the gas connections deserve care. If you ever smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility. Columbia Gas of Kentucky publishes its 24/7 emergency number as 1-800-432-9515 on its website, and the same number is worth saving to your phone if Columbia Gas serves your address. Check your bill to confirm your utility.

Permit requirements vary by location and by type of work, so ask your building department what applies. Model plumbing and fuel gas standards set the rules for venting and clearances, and states adopt and amend them, so confirm which edition applies at your address. A licensed plumber should know the local steps and should tell you who handles them.

Maintenance after the install

Once a year, flush a tank heater to clear sediment, test the relief valve and check the anode rod. Tankless units need descaling on a schedule that depends on water hardness. Frankfort Plant Board's published water quality report does not list hardness, so ask the utility or test your tap, then set the interval to match.

If a problem shows up later, our water heater repair page covers diagnosis and the common failed parts. Keep the date of installation handy, since it helps us judge wear.
