About Frankfort 24/7 Plumbing

A plumbing dispatch built around the one thing that matters in an emergency: someone answers.

Call (855) 575-2890

Frankfort 24/7 Plumbing is a plumbing service for the capital city and the towns around it. The business is built around a simple idea. When a pipe bursts at two in the morning, you should reach a person who asks the right questions and sends a plumber. The dispatch line is open around the clock, every day of the year.

We serve Frankfort, Versailles, Lawrenceburg and Midway. We take calls and emails only, and there is no walk-in office. We handle drains, water heaters, leaks, sewer lines, burst pipes, sump pumps and the planned work behind them.

How we work

We start by listening. A caller who says the toilet is overflowing might have a simple clog or a failing main, and the questions we ask are meant to tell the two apart. We tell you how to limit damage while a plumber is on the way, and we give you a clear price before work starts.

We tell you what we find, including the findings that mean you do not need the larger job. If a repair is not worth doing, we say so. If a repair can wait until morning, we say that too. See our services page for the full list.

What you can expect from us

  • A person answers the phone at any hour, including holidays.
  • A clear price before work starts, in writing for planned jobs.
  • Ask any plumber about their license. Kentucky licenses plumbers through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, and you can verify any plumber's license with the department.
  • Ask about timing when you call.
  • Honest advice about repair versus replacement.

Rooted in the Frankfort area

Frankfort has been Kentucky's capital since 1792, and its streets run from the historic Capitol district to the Kentucky River. We know the older neighborhoods and their older pipes, and we know the cold snaps and the river that shape plumbing here. We also serve Versailles, Lawrenceburg and Midway, and each of those towns has its own page.

How a call becomes a repair

  1. Someone answers

    The dispatch line is staffed at every hour. You describe the problem, and we ask a few direct questions about where the water is, what is affected and whether anyone is at risk.

  2. You get immediate steps

    Before a plumber arrives, we tell you what to shut off, what to move and what to leave alone. Those steps often decide how much damage a house takes.

  3. A plumber is dispatched

    The crew that goes out carries equipment matched to what you described, such as drain machines, a camera, pipe tools or a water heater.

  4. Diagnosis and price

    The plumber finds the cause, explains it and gives you a clear price before work starts.

  5. Repair, test and explain

    The repair is tested before the crew leaves, and you are told what to watch for in the days after.

What we do and what we refer out

We handle plumbing: drains, sewer lines, water supply, water heaters, fixtures, sump pumps and the drainage that keeps basements dry. When a problem sits outside plumbing, we say so. A cracked foundation wall belongs to a foundation contractor, a failed furnace to an HVAC company and a tripped panel to an electrician.

That honesty is deliberate. A homeowner with water in the basement needs to know whether the answer is a sump pump or a structural repair, and a plumber who sells the wrong fix wastes your money and your time. The pages on sump pumps and basement waterproofing explain where the plumbing side ends.

Licensing and how to check it

Kentucky licenses master and journeyman plumbers through the Department of Housing, Buildings and Construction, Division of Plumbing, under 815 KAR 20:030. You can ask any plumber for a license and verify it with the department. We encourage you to do that with every contractor you hire, including us.

Permit and inspection rules depend on the job and the address, so ask your local building department what applies before major work. If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility first. Columbia Gas of Kentucky lists its 24/7 emergency number as 1-800-432-9515.

The places we know

Frankfort sits in the Kentucky River valley and has been the capital since 1792. The river has flooded the city repeatedly, with a record crest of 48.47 feet in December 1978 that covered more than half of downtown, according to FEMA's high water mark pilot summary. Winters bring January lows near 25 degrees Fahrenheit at the airport, and those two facts shape the plumbing problems we see most.

Outside Frankfort, Versailles is the Woodford County seat and sits in horse country, Lawrenceburg is the Anderson County seat and a bourbon town, and Midway is a small railroad town on Interstate 64. Each has a different mix of older houses, farms and newer homes, so each gets its own page and its own local detail.
